From FY2019 to FY2024, every adopted budget planned a draw on reserves, and every year came in better than planned: in each year, actual state revenue ran above the adopted budget and spending came in below the final amended budget. FY2025 broke the pattern. The FY2026 and FY2027 budgets plan the smallest draws in the series; neither has been tested by an audit.

**FY2025 (audited).** HISD budgeted $80 million in revenue from property sales and actually received **$30,000**. Spending ran $74.7 million over the district's own final amended budget, driven by instructional salaries, the Texas Connections Academy virtual school, school leadership, and recapture.

- The adopted budget started from an assumed $799.9 million beginning fund balance. The audited figure turned out to be $748.1 million, so the adopted budget overstated its own starting reserves by about $52 million. Amendment #1 reset the starting point to the audited figure.
- Amendment #1 added a one-time $46.0 million "prior period property value audit" revenue item (raised to $46.6 million in Amendment #2). Without it, the final FY2026 budget would show a $64.7 million draw; without it and the $15 million of budgeted property sales, a $79.7 million draw, other things unchanged.
- Budgeted state revenue fell from $414.5 million (adopted) to $377.3 million (Amendment #2), and budgeted transportation spending rose from $38.3 million to $52.8 million.
- At March 31, 2026, against the February budget, actual revenue was $1.880 billion and actual spending $1.394 billion (HISD's April 2026 workshop). Spending is weighted toward the end of the year, so these partial-year figures do not indicate the full-year result.

<p class="caption">Sources: audited ACFR balance sheets, FY2019-FY2025; HISD's FY2026 Budget Amendment #2 and FY2027 adopted budget. Months of spending: fund balance divided by one month of actual total General Fund spending (budgeted spending for 2026 and 2027); this is not the board policy's formula (see below).</p>

- **The unassigned balance was held exactly flat for two years** — exactly $590,067,862 in both 2023 and 2024 — while an "Instructional Reserve" accounting assignment absorbed the real losses, falling from $264.2 million to $215.6 million to $0. Looking at the unassigned figure alone, without also watching the assigned and total balances, would have missed the real drawdown.
- **HISD's unassigned balance is below the board's three-month goal.** Board policy CE(LOCAL), adopted November 2021, sets a *goal* of an unassigned balance of at least three months of operating expenditures. It measures that as adopted General Fund operating expenditures, less recapture and non-cash expenditures, divided by 12, times three. HISD's April 2026 budget workshop put three months at $548 million for FY2025. Applying the policy's formula to the adopted FY2025 budget, less the $103 million of TRS on-behalf payments, gives about $499 million (our calculation; HISD has not published its own). The June 2025 unassigned balance was $464.8 million, $34.6-83.2 million short. The 2.5 months in the table above uses actual total spending, not the policy's formula. HISD's budgets do not split the projected 2026 and 2027 balances into assigned and unassigned portions.
- **A widely repeated figure that HISD's reserves "fell about $450 million" is directionally right but matches no single audited line.** Depending on the measure: total General Fund balance fell $379 million; the unrestricted fund balance (committed + assigned + unassigned) fell $501 million; all governmental funds combined fell $546 million.
- HISD's own financial consultants (Alvarez & Marsal) projected the district's fund balance would have fallen to $252 million by FY2026 if no budget cuts had been made.
