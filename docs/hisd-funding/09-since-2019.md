# 9. Seven years: FY2019 to FY2025

The earlier sections focus on the three years since the state takeover in June 2023. This section starts in school year 2018-19 (HISD's FY2019): before COVID, before the 2019 school-finance overhaul (HB 3), and before federal COVID relief (ESSER). Sources are HISD's audited reports for FY2019 through FY2025, TEA's Summary of Finances for every school year from 2018-19 to 2026-27, and TEA's actual-spending (PEIMS) and staffing (TAPR) data.

<p class="caption">Source: HISD audited ACFRs FY2019-FY2025 (General Fund statements; enrollment from the statistical section). Per-student spending is General Fund expenditure minus the recapture expense, divided by enrollment. Data: <code>data/hisd_history_gf_fy2019_2025.csv</code>.</p>

- **Surpluses, then deficits.** HISD's General Fund ran a surplus in each of FY2019-FY2022, adding $463M to reserves in total. The three deficits since (FY2023-FY2025) total $497M, more than erasing them.
- **Reserves are back below FY2019.** The General Fund balance peaked at $1.127 billion in June 2023 and was $748.1 million in June 2025, below the $819.0 million of June 2019.
- **Spending per student rose 51% while enrollment fell 16%.** General Fund spending (excluding recapture) went from $8,228 to $12,415 per enrolled student. Enrollment fell by 33,045 students (−15.8%).
- **Budgets beaten for six years, then missed.** Each year from FY2019 to FY2024, HISD's actual General Fund result was better than its adopted budget. FY2025 was the first year it came in worse: the adopted budget planned a $131.1M fund-balance draw, and the actual draw was $299.1M, $168.0M more.

Before federal COVID relief, **HISD spent less per student than the state average** (5% less in 2018-19, 7% less in 2019-20). It passed the state average in 2022-23 and was 9% above it by 2024-25. Spending on campus leadership was already above the state rate in 2018-19 ($697 per student vs $589) but grew much faster: $1,369 vs $752 by 2024-25. Instructional leadership was below the state rate in 2018-19 ($153 vs $162) and 2.1 times it by 2024-25 ($474 vs $227).

- **Formula funding per student moved in two steps.** HB 3 (2019) raised it by about $1,000 per attending student. It then stayed roughly flat for five years before HB 2 (2025) raised it by about $700.
- **HISD paid $969 million in recapture over 2018-19 to 2024-25**, by TEA's final figures. The payment fell to zero after the 2023 tax-relief law: TEA's first projection for 2023-24 (June 2023) showed HISD owing $335.2M; the final shows $0.
- **Recapture estimates move a lot before they settle.** TEA re-issues each year's report many times (28 to 66 runs per year) and keeps adjusting "Final" figures for up to six years. Between first projection and final settlement, HISD's recapture moved by $100-370 million in most years. HISD's audited recapture expense reflects the estimate current when its books close each September, so it can differ from the final settlement; for FY2019, HISD booked $80.1M more than TEA's final figure.

The campuses chosen for NES already cost about 13% more per student before the reform, mostly because they serve more disadvantaged students. About $3,400-4,300 per student of today's $5,095 gap arrived with NES itself. Enrollment at those campuses fell from 91,708 (2018-19) to 69,459 (2024-25), down 24%, against a 9% decline at campuses never in NES.

## Federal money before and after ESSER

Total federal spending (single audits) was $345.2M in FY2018, including $29.8M of Hurricane Harvey recovery, and $325.9M in FY2019. It peaked at $836.6M in FY2023 with ESSER and was back to $389.6M by FY2025, close to the pre-ESSER level. The General Fund absorbed costs that ESSER had carried, which is the main reason the deficits appeared when ESSER ended (see [Section 3](03-where-the-money-goes.md)).

## Audit findings

HISD's audits for FY2019, FY2020 and FY2021 each reported a material weakness in financial-reporting controls. FY2019's cited board governance (during the TEA investigation that led to the takeover) and an improper year-end cutoff of about $7.2M. FY2020's cited unrecorded capital leases and goods receipts. FY2021's cited in-kind revenue and expenditures. The FY2021 weakness capped HISD's state financial-integrity (FIRST) rating at C (79) for 2021-22; the FY2020 one did the same for 2020-21. The FY2023-FY2025 audits reported no material weaknesses.
