# 9. FY2019 to FY2027: seven audited years and two budgeted

The earlier sections focus on the three years since the state takeover in June 2023. This section starts in school year 2018-19 (HISD's FY2019): before COVID, before the 2019 school-finance overhaul (HB 3), and before federal COVID relief (ESSER). Sources are HISD's audited reports for FY2019 through FY2025, TEA's Summary of Finances for every school year from 2018-19 to 2026-27, and TEA's actual-spending (PEIMS) and staffing (TAPR) data. FY2026 and FY2027 are not yet audited; for those years the tables show HISD's budgets and forecasts, TEA's estimates and TEA's enrollment counts, each labeled.

- **Surpluses, then deficits.** HISD's General Fund ran a surplus in each of FY2019-FY2022, adding $463M to reserves in total. The three deficits since (FY2023-FY2025) total $497M, more than erasing them.
- **Reserves are back below FY2019.** The General Fund balance peaked at $1.127 billion in June 2023 and was $748.1 million in June 2025, below the $819.0 million of June 2019.
- **Spending per student rose 51% while enrollment fell 16%.** General Fund spending (excluding recapture) went from $8,228 to $12,415 per enrolled student. Consumer prices (CPI-U) rose about 26% from June 2019 to June 2025, so the rise is roughly 20% after inflation; CPI is not a school-cost index. Enrollment fell by 33,045 students (−15.8%).
- **Budgets beaten for six years, then missed.** Each year from FY2019 to FY2024, HISD's actual General Fund result was better than its adopted budget. FY2025 was the first year it came in worse: the adopted budget planned a $131.1M fund-balance draw, and the actual draw was $299.1M, $168.0M more.
- **The FY2026 and FY2027 budgets plan near-balanced years.** The final FY2026 budget plans an $18.0M draw, after a one-time $46.6M property-value audit payment; the FY2027 budget plans $0.8M. Both rest on cuts (FY2027 instruction is budgeted $113.9M below FY2025's actual) and on continued enrollment decline. Neither is audited; see [Section 5](05-budget-deficit-and-reserves.md).

Before federal COVID relief, **HISD spent less per student than the state average** (5% less in 2018-19, 7% less in 2019-20). It passed the state average in 2022-23 and was 9% above it by 2024-25. Spending on campus leadership was already above the state rate in 2018-19 ($697 per student vs $589) but grew much faster: $1,369 vs $752 by 2024-25. Instructional leadership was below the state rate in 2018-19 ($153 vs $162) and 2.1 times it by 2024-25 ($474 vs $227).

- **Formula funding per student moved in two steps.** It rose $860 per attending student with HB 3 (2019), then stayed between $9,404 and $9,894 for five years. In 2025-26 it rose $1,170 to $11,064 (TEA estimate). That second step combines new HB 2 (2025) allotments with more of HISD's own tax rate counting as Tier Two enrichment ([Section 4](04-state-funding-per-student.md)).
- **HISD paid $969 million in recapture over 2018-19 to 2024-25**, by TEA's final figures; HISD's audited recapture expense for FY2019-FY2025 totals $1,061.7 million, because it books TEA's estimate at year-end and later settlements differ. The payment fell to zero after the 2023 tax-relief law: TEA's first projection for 2023-24 (June 2023) showed HISD owing $335.2M; the final shows $0.
- **Recapture estimates move a lot before they settle.** TEA re-issues each year's report many times (28 to 66 runs per year) and keeps adjusting "Final" figures for up to six years. Between first projection and final settlement, HISD's recapture moved by $100-370 million in most years. HISD's audited recapture expense reflects the estimate current when its books close each September, so it can differ from the final settlement; for FY2019, HISD booked $80.1M more than TEA's final figure.

## Federal money before and after ESSER

Total federal spending (single audits) was $345.2M in FY2018, including $29.8M of Hurricane Harvey recovery, and $325.9M in FY2019. It peaked at $836.6M in FY2023 with ESSER and was back to $389.6M by FY2025, close to the pre-ESSER level. There is no audited figure yet for FY2026; HISD's FY2027 budget materials list $177.3M of federal grant planning amounts, plus about $124M of budgeted child-nutrition revenue. The audited statements are consistent with the General Fund absorbing costs that ESSER had carried. From FY2023 to FY2025, grant-funded instruction fell $219 million while General Fund instruction rose $225 million. That shift is a major reason the deficits appeared when ESSER ended, although which positions moved would take payroll records to show (see [Section 3](03-where-the-money-goes.md)).

## Audit findings

HISD's audits for FY2019, FY2020 and FY2021 each reported a material weakness in financial-reporting controls. FY2019's cited board governance (during the TEA investigation that led to the takeover) and an improper year-end cutoff of about $7.2M. FY2020's cited unrecorded capital leases and goods receipts. FY2021's cited in-kind revenue and expenditures. The FY2021 weakness capped HISD's state financial-integrity (FIRST) rating at C (79) for 2021-22; the FY2020 one did the same for 2020-21. The FY2023-FY2025 audits reported no material weaknesses; the FY2025 audit reported three significant deficiencies ([Section 7](07-federal-funds-debt.md)).
