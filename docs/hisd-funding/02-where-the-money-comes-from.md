**No all-funds figure exists yet for FY2026 or FY2027.** HISD's board adopts budgets only for the General, Debt Service and Nutrition funds; federal grants, which pass through a separate Special Revenue fund, are not part of any adopted budget. HISD's FY2027 budget materials list $177.3 million of federal grant planning amounts (Title I $112.7 million, special education $37.6 million and others), but these are planning amounts, not a budget or a result.

Property tax revenue fell by about $300 million in FY2024. The cause was state-ordered tax-rate compression (HISD's maintenance-and-operations rate fell from $0.8705 to $0.7016 per $100 of value) and the homestead exemption rising from $40,000 to $100,000 — both from 2023 state property-tax relief legislation. Federal revenue more than doubled in FY2022-FY2024 with federal COVID relief (ESSER) and fell back by FY2025.

FY2026's budgeted state revenue moved down twice during the year: $414.5 million as adopted (June 2025), $398.3 million in the February 2026 amendment, and $377.3 million in the June 2026 amendment. In every audited year, FY2019 to FY2025, HISD's actual General Fund state revenue came in above its adopted budget (by $184.7 million in FY2019 and $47.8 million in FY2025). Its FY2024 budget was adopted before the 2023 tax-relief law and overstated property tax revenue by about $366 million.

A commonly repeated "74% local / 14% state / 12% federal" split is HISD's **all-funds** figure for FY2027, which includes about $177 million of federal grants that flow through a separate fund. On a General Fund basis — the money that actually pays for day-to-day instruction — federal money is only about 1%.

HISD's total operating revenue per student is exactly the state average. All TEA local figures here are after recapture: TEA reports M&O property tax "excluding recapture." Property tax plus state funds is $9,550 per enrolled student; adding other local revenue gives $10,391 of nonfederal operating revenue. HISD is a property-wealthy district by Texas's school-finance standards, so the state covers only about 10% of its operating revenue — against 45% statewide for an average district. The mechanics of why are in [Section 4](04-state-funding-per-student.md).
