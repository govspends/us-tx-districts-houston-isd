- **FY2019 to FY2025 (audited):** General Fund instruction rose 30%, school leadership 65%, and instructional leadership more than tripled, most of it from FY2023 on, while enrollment fell 16%. General Fund spending per student, excluding recapture and TIRZ payments, rose 52%. Consumer prices (CPI-U, June 2019 to June 2025) rose about 26% over the same period.
- **Part of the General Fund growth is costs moving off federal grants.** From FY2023 to FY2025, General Fund instruction rose 21.7%, but instruction across all governmental funds rose only 0.4% ($1,427.3M to $1,432.5M), because grant-funded instruction fell from $390.0M to $170.6M as ESSER ended. Leadership grew across all funds too: school leadership 43.9% ($168.4M to $242.3M) and instructional leadership 91.8% ($43.8M to $83.9M). Which positions moved from grants to the General Fund cannot be told from these statements. (Source: governmental funds statements of revenues, expenditures and changes in fund balances, FY2023 and FY2025 ACFRs.)
- **FY2026 and FY2027 (budgets):** HISD plans to cut instruction to $1,148.0 million in FY2027, $113.9 million below FY2025's actual. School leadership is budgeted about $19 million below FY2025, and instructional leadership keeps growing. Neither year is audited. In FY2025, actual General Fund spending came in $150.7 million over the adopted budget and $74.7 million over the final amended budget.

Across **all** governmental funds (General, Special Revenue, Debt Service, Capital Projects), total spending was $3.472 billion (FY2023), $3.441 billion (FY2024) and $3.151 billion (FY2025) — falling, because grant-funded (largely ESSER COVID-relief) spending fell sharply. The General Fund, which pays for ongoing operations, kept growing.

**Payroll is 77-79% of General Fund spending** ($1.742 billion in FY2025). Contracted services were $336.9 million in FY2025, including recapture. HISD's budget books allocate $55.9 million (FY2025) and $67.2 million (FY2026) to contracted charter schools and its virtual school option, mostly Texas Connections Academy. TEA's campus data shows Texas Connections alone spent $56.5 million from the General Fund in 2024-25.

**Campus administrators rose 55%** while enrollment fell 7% over the same period. Librarian positions fell from 154 to 50 budgeted, and social worker positions from 34 to 12, between FY2024 and FY2026. Central-office staffing was cut by about 1,500 positions between November 2023 and June 2024, with further cuts of about 348 FTEs (FY2026) and 205 positions (FY2027).

HISD pays the highest average teacher salary among the eight peer districts examined. Its new pay-for-performance salary scale runs $70,000 to $92,000, with teachers at NES campuses receiving an additional $6,000 to $9,000. 7.0% of HISD's teachers have no college degree, versus a 2.4% state average.
