Across **all** governmental funds (General, Special Revenue, Debt Service, Capital Projects), total spending was $3.472 billion (FY2023), $3.441 billion (FY2024) and $3.151 billion (FY2025) — falling, because grant-funded (largely ESSER COVID-relief) spending fell sharply. The General Fund, which pays for ongoing operations, kept growing.

**Payroll is 77-79% of General Fund spending** ($1.742 billion in FY2025). Contracted services were $336.9 million in FY2025, including recapture. Spending on contracted charter schools and HISD's virtual school option (mostly Texas Connections Academy) was $55.9 million in FY2025, budgeted to rise to $67.2 million in FY2026.

**Campus administrators rose 55%** while enrollment fell 7% over the same period. Librarian positions fell from 154 to 50 budgeted, and social worker positions from 34 to 12, between FY2024 and FY2026. Central-office staffing was cut by about 1,500 positions between November 2023 and June 2024, with further cuts of about 348 FTEs (FY2026) and 205 positions (FY2027).

HISD pays the highest average teacher salary among the eight peer districts examined. Its new pay-for-performance salary scale runs $70,000 to $92,000, with teachers at NES campuses receiving an additional $6,000 to $9,000. 7.0% of HISD's teachers have no college degree, versus a 2.4% state average.
