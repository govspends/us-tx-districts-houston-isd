# 7. Federal funds, debt and other money flows

## Federal funding and the ESSER cliff

| | FY2022 | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Total federal spending | $699.4M | $836.6M | $736.7M | $389.6M |
| ESSER (COVID relief) | $374.2M | $441.4M | $316.9M | $40.9M |
| Title I | | | $167.2M | $126.6M |
| Child nutrition | | | $135.2M | $129.8M |
| Special education (IDEA-B) | | | $59.6M | $42.5M |

<p class="caption">Source: HISD's single-audit Schedule of Expenditures of Federal Awards, FY2022-FY2025.</p>

HISD's total ESSER allocation across all three rounds was $1.245 billion (ESSER I $82.0M, II $358.2M, III $804.8M), compared with $347.7 million of total federal spending in FY2020 — a large, temporary expansion of federal money that was always going to end. ESSER I did not add net new money: the state used it to offset HISD's regular state aid, and TEA separately reduced HISD's state aid by $65.9 million to "hold it harmless" for attendance losses under ESSER III. An internal audit in 2024 found that only $34.7 million of $73.8 million in tested "learning loss" spending could be traced to documented support, and HISD's FY2025 single audit found an equipment-inventory failure (20 of 25 sampled assets unaccounted for) along with $54,626 in questioned costs.

## Debt and liabilities

| | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Bonds and notes outstanding | $2,123.2M | $1,813.6M | **$1,513.6M** ($8,565 per student) |
| Net pension liability (Teacher Retirement System) | $709.9M | $985.3M | $768.0M |
| Net retiree-health (OPEB) liability | $355.8M | $382.6M | $502.5M |

<p class="caption">Source: HISD's audited ACFR, FY2023-FY2025.</p>

- **Credit ratings:** Moody's Aaa, S&P AA+ on HISD's underlying tax-supported debt, with no downgrade since the 2023 state takeover.
- **Variable-rate debt was fully retired in May 2025**, with $115.4 million paid off early using cash on hand.
- **A 2026 refinancing** (the $342.8 million Series 2026 Limited Tax Refunding Bonds) compresses roughly 12 years of bond repayment into about 4. As a result, total debt service jumps to **$438.5 million in FY2027** — more than the roughly $365 million the dedicated debt-service tax raises in a year. The gap is being covered from the debt-service fund's accumulated balance.
- **The November 2024 bond election failed.** Proposition A ($3.96 billion) failed 57.9% to 42.1%; Proposition B ($440 million) failed 58.2% to 41.8%. HISD's own pre-election estimate of total cost including interest, had it passed, was $8.9 billion.
- **Borrowing has continued since, without another voter election**, using debt instruments that don't require one: $114.4 million of maintenance-tax notes issued in July 2025 (5% interest initially, stepping to 7% if not remarketed by 2028), plus board authorization for up to $182.5 million more in lease-revenue bonds for a career-and-technical-education center.
- **Tax rates fell** from $1.2067 per $100 of value (FY2017-2019) to $0.8683 (FY2024-2025), driven by the state compression described in [Section 4](04-state-funding-per-student.md). The debt-service (interest & sinking) portion of the rate has been flat at $0.1667 since FY2018.

## Other money flows

- **Tax Increment Reinvestment Zones (TIRZ).** HISD pays a portion of its property-tax collections into 12 City of Houston tax-increment zones: $48.4 million (FY2024) and $37.9 million (FY2025). It received $33.4 million and $54.5 million back over the same two years.
- **Vendor contract approvals.** About $870 million in vendor agreements were put in place without the board approval normally required, and were ratified after the fact in January 2025, according to press reporting.
- **School closures.** The board voted in February 2026 to close 12 schools; the district has not disclosed the projected savings.
- **State vouchers.** 1,207 HISD students received Texas Education Freedom Account vouchers under the state's new program, an estimated $9.7 million in per-student funding that would otherwise have gone to HISD, according to advocacy-group estimates citing state Comptroller data.
