**FY2026 and FY2027 (not audited).** The FY2026 single audit is part of the FY2026 ACFR, not yet published. HISD's budget materials give only planning amounts for federal grants: $190.4 million for FY2025 (ESSA grants, with $0 of ESSER, against $323.9 million of ESSER in FY2024) and $177.3 million for FY2027 (Title I $112.7 million, special education $37.6 million, Title II $9.9 million, Title IV $7.9 million, Title III $5.8 million, Perkins $2.8 million, preschool special education $0.6 million). No planning total for FY2026 was found. Budgeted federal child-nutrition revenue is $130.9 million for FY2026 (amended, June 2026) and $124.0 million for FY2027 (adopted).

HISD's total ESSER allocation across all three rounds was $1.245 billion (ESSER I $82.0M, II $358.2M, III $804.8M), compared with $347.7 million of total federal spending in FY2020 — a large, temporary expansion of federal money that was always going to end. ESSER I did not add net new money: the state used it to offset HISD's regular state aid, and TEA separately reduced HISD's state aid by $65.9 million to "hold it harmless" for attendance losses under ESSER III. An internal audit in May 2024 (Whitley Penn) tested four ESSER III "learning loss" initiatives totaling $73.8 million in HISD's tracking tool and could trace only $34.7 million to supporting documents. The auditors attributed the gap to initiatives that had changed since the tracker was built, and found "no indication" that HISD was at risk of missing the federal requirement to spend 20% of ESSER III on learning loss. It is a documentation exception, not evidence of missing money.

HISD's FY2025 single audit reported three significant deficiencies:

- **Equipment (finding 2025-002):** 20 of 25 sampled federally funded assets were "unaccounted for" *or* excluded from the inventory counts. Six were HVAC units left out of the counts, and about 45% of campuses did not fully take part in the inventory. No questioned costs. The finding does not show that the assets are physically missing.
- **TCLAS subscriptions (2025-003):** $54,626 of questioned costs for service periods beyond the grant's liquidation period.
- **Medicaid school health revenue, SHARS (2025-001, financial reporting):** HISD's estimated SHARS revenue and accrual were misstated because the state's 2023 cost-settlement report was not considered.

- **Credit ratings:** Moody's Aaa and S&P AA+ on HISD's underlying tax-supported debt in both the FY2024 and FY2025 ACFRs. Moody's rating on HISD's Public Facility Corporation lease-revenue bonds is shown as Aaa in the FY2024 ACFR and Aa1 in the FY2025 ACFR; whether that is a downgrade or a correction was not confirmed.
- **Variable-rate debt was eliminated in May 2025, then reintroduced.** On May 1, 2025, HISD refunded all its variable-rate bonds into fixed-rate bonds (Series 2025B), and on May 28 it paid off $115.4 million of other bonds early with debt-service cash. In July 2025 it issued $114.4 million of variable-rate maintenance tax notes, at 5% fixed until a 2028 tender.
- **A 2026 refinancing** (the $342.8 million Series 2026 Limited Tax Refunding Bonds) compresses roughly 12 years of bond repayment into about 4. As a result, total debt service jumps to **$438.5 million in FY2027**: $418.2 million on bonds paid from the debt-service (I&S) tax and $20.3 million on maintenance-tax notes paid from the General Fund (Series 2026 Official Statement, Table 8). The adopted FY2027 Debt Service Fund budget covers its $444.4 million of spending with $399.5 million of tax and state revenue, $26.1 million of transfers in, a $4.8 million refunding premium, and $14.0 million of fund balance ($187.2 million to $173.2 million). The official statement and the budget schedule differ in scope and timing, and were not reconciled issue by issue.
- **The November 2024 bond election failed.** Proposition A ($3.96 billion) failed 57.9% to 42.1%; Proposition B ($440 million) failed 58.2% to 41.8%. HISD's own pre-election estimate of total cost including interest, had it passed, was $8.9 billion.
- **Borrowing has continued since, without another voter election**, using debt instruments that don't require one: $114.4 million of maintenance-tax notes issued in July 2025 (5% interest initially, stepping to 7% if not remarketed by 2028), plus board authorization for up to $182.5 million more in lease-revenue bonds for a career-and-technical-education center.
- **Tax rates fell** from $1.2067 per $100 of value (FY2017-2019) to $0.8683 (FY2024-2025), driven by the state compression described in [Section 4](04-state-funding-per-student.md). For FY2026 (tax year 2025) HISD adopted $0.8783, including one cent of "disaster pennies" added in October 2025 (a May 2026 bond disclosure shows $0.8489; see [Section 8](08-figures-that-disagree.md)); for FY2027 (tax year 2026) it proposed $0.8421, which it had not yet posted as adopted on October 8, 2026. The debt-service (interest & sinking) portion of the rate has been flat at $0.1667 since FY2018.

## Other money flows

- **Tax Increment Reinvestment Zones (TIRZ).** HISD pays a portion of its property-tax collections into 12 City of Houston tax-increment zones: $48.4 million (FY2024) and $37.9 million (FY2025). It received $33.4 million and $54.5 million back over the same two years.
- **Vendor contract approvals.** Vendor agreements with spending ceilings of up to $870 million were put in place without the board approval normally required, and were ratified after the fact in January 2025, according to press reporting. These are approval ceilings, not amounts paid, and the reporting does not establish any loss.
- **School closures.** The board voted in February 2026 to close 12 schools. HISD's May 2026 budget workshop projects a $15 million saving from "consolidate schools" in FY2027; it has not published a school-by-school estimate, transition costs or realized savings.
- **State vouchers.** 1,207 HISD students received Texas Education Freedom Account vouchers under the state's new program, an estimated $9.7 million in per-student funding that would otherwise have gone to HISD, according to advocacy-group estimates citing state Comptroller data. This is an unverified estimate: it assumes every voucher student would otherwise have attended HISD.
