## (c) Local-vs-state share / recapture
- HISD paid $55.5M recapture in 2024-25 — 10th-highest in Texas (Texas Tribune, "What to know about Robin Hood," 2026-07-24: https://www.texastribune.org/2026/07/24/what-to-know-texas-school-finance-robin-hood-funding/). HISD IS a recapture payer — correcting an earlier wrong assumption.
- Statewide recapture: $3B in 2024-25 from 225 districts, up from $1.4B a decade earlier; ~$45.5B cumulative since 1994.
- Tier 1 formula confirmed via Texas Tribune (2026-09-21, https://www.texastribune.org/2026/09/21/houston-isd-state-takeover-sustainability/): WADA × Basic Allotment. Funding tracks attendance, not enrollment or need.
- FY2026-27 taxable property value in HISD: $232.62B, +0.24% YoY (essentially flat).
- FY2026-27 tax rate: $0.8421/$100, down from $0.8783/$100 (state-mandated compression).
- Median homeowner tax bill: $2,608 → $2,530 despite median home value rising ~$3,500 (rate compression offsetting value growth).
- General fund revenue mix (~$2B budget): local 74%, state 14%, federal 12%.
- Conclusion: "rising local value crowds out state share" does not appear to be HISD's primary dynamic (local value nearly flat); enrollment/WADA decline is the dominant driver of falling state aid.
- Source for budget/tax figures: Houston Chronicle budget article (WebSearch synthesis only, not independently fetched — JS/paywall blocked direct fetch): https://www.houstonchronicle.com/news/houston-texas/education/hisd/article/budget-2026-2027-22317443.php
- Defender Network budget-deficit article mentions "decline in recapture redistribution funding tied to lower enrollment" — phrasing ambiguous, UNVERIFIED, could reflect summarization error: https://defendernetwork.com/news/education/hisd-budget-narrowing-gap-campus-reform/

## (d) NES (New Education System) funding differential
- NES campuses: base funding ~$8,566/student (Chronicle) or $8,459 (Tribune, FY26-27 projection); total incl. add-ons ~$10,635/student; special ed add-on $1,696.
- Non-NES campuses: base funding $6,133/student (Chronicle) or $6,265 (Tribune); total ~$7,103/student; special ed add-on $717.
- Gap characterized as "~$2,500 per student" between NES and non-NES (Chronicle headline).
- First-year NES campus conversion cost: ~$700,000-$704,000/campus; ongoing differential ~$2,200/student/year.
- Texas Tribune: HISD received NO additional net-new state dollars for the takeover itself — NES is funded by reallocating HISD's existing (local+state blend) budget, not a distinct state allotment.
- HISD reserves fell from ~7 months' to ~3 months' operating cushion, partly funding this internal reallocation.
- No TEA statutory citation found for "NES" as a named FSP allotment line (unlike the real Teacher Incentive Allotment or CCMR outcomes bonus) — likely an internal HISD budget category, not a state program. UNVERIFIED — needs a direct check of TEA's FSP allotment list.

## (e) Explicit "decrease" statements and true cause
- No source states the per-student RATE fell for HISD. All cited decreases = enrollment decline × flat-to-slightly-rising rate.
- SY2025-26: HISD enrollment fell 8,300+ students (-4.7%), vs. district's own projection of -6,700 (Houston Chronicle, Nov 2025 — not independently refetched, JS-blocked).
- At ~$6,160/student × 8,300 ≈ $51M lost (Houston Public Media synthesis, not independently refetched — blocked on fetch): https://www.houstonpublicmedia.org/articles/education/2025/11/24/537011/hisd-enrollment-decline-school-funding-texas-education-agency/
- SY2026-27: projected loss of ~4,000 students × $6,215/student (HISD's own FY27 budget messaging per Chronicle) ≈ $50-55M projected state-aid loss.
- Cumulative since 2023 takeover: ~21,000 fewer students, ~$190M cumulative state-aid loss over 3 years (LatinTimes + Texas Tribune, consistent, both using WADA × Basic Allotment methodology).
- Year-by-year enrollment loss (LatinTimes): ~5,800 (2023-24) → ~7,400 (2024-25) → ~8,000-8,300 (2025-26) — accelerating, not a one-time blip.
- Two causes identified by Texas Tribune for the enrollment decline itself:
  1. Immigration-enforcement-related flight: immigrant-family enrollment down ~22%.
  2. Flight specifically from NES-reform campuses: -9.7% vs. -1.2% at non-NES campuses — i.e., the takeover/reform itself appears to be partly driving the enrollment loss that then reduces state funding (a self-reinforcing dynamic).
