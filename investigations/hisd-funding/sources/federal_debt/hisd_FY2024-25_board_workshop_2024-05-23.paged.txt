23: Challenges of State Funding in Texas Education
23: State Funding Shortfall
23: • School districts in Texas are grappling with staff reductions and program cuts due to stagnant state investments.
23: • Over the past five years, state education funding has not kept up with inflation, resulting in a 20% decrease in
23:    purchasing power for districts.
23: Ranking in Education Spending
23: • Texas ranks among the lowest 10 states in education spending according to a recent study by the Albert Shanker
23:    Institute, University of Miami, and Rutgers University.
23: District Budget Challenges
23: • Several major Texas school districts are facing significant budget shortfalls:
23:       • Austin ISD: $89 million deficit leading to 41 central office job cuts.
23:       • Spring Branch ISD: Eliminating 305 positions with central office cuts and program changes.
23:       • Frisco ISD: $30.81 million shortfall impacting programs and services.
23: Legislative Action
23: • Houston-area legislators urging the Texas Education Agency to utilize surplus funds to help districts facing budget gaps.
23: Call to Action
23: • Executive Director of Texas School Alliance emphasizes the urgent need for increased state investment in public
23:    education to support teachers and students.

29:    Calculating non-NES Campuses
29: • Budgets in 23-24 per student allocation for campuses was based on weights developed by the
29:   previous administration. This was the process in previous years.
29: • Budgets in 24-25 took the weighted per student allocation multiplied it by the campus projected
29:   enrollment and multiplied by average daily attendance (ADA).
29: Example:
29: Step 1. The SY 23-24 per student allocation for a particular campus was $7,000
29: Step 2. For SY 24-25, the $7000 was multiplied by the current enrollment (800 students) as of
29: March 5, 2024. This would equal $5,600,000.
29: Step 3. The result of step 2 was multiplied by the campus ADA percentage of 91.5%.
29:                                    $5,600,000 * 91.5% = $5,124,000
29: Step 4. The allocation in step 3 is an 8.5% reduction from the allocation calculated in step 2. If the
29: reduction exceeded 12%, it was capped.

53:  Nutrition Services Programs
53: Summary of Nutrition Services
53: The Nutrition Services Department oversees the preparation, distribution, and service of nutritious meals each
53: day, fueling our students' academic performance and inspiring the development of life-long dietary health and
53: wellness behaviors. This oversight includes the administration of multiple Federal, State and Local child nutrition
53: programs such as:
53: • School Breakfast Program (SBP)
53: • National School Lunch Program (NSLP)
53: • After School Care Program (ASCP)
53: • Fresh Fruit and Vegetables Program (FFVP)
53: • Farm to School Program (FTS)
53: • SMART Snack Program
53: • Child and Adult Care Food Program (CACFP)
53: • Summer Food Service Program (SFSP)
53: • Seamless Summer Option (SSO)
53: • USDA Foods Program
