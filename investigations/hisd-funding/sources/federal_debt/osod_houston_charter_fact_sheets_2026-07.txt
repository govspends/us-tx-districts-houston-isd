  Charter Schools: Impact on Houston
                            Houston ISD lost an estimated
                    $470,317,724 to charter schools just in 2024-25.


    A public school district can’t simply cut costs dollar-for-dollar to the loss in
    revenue when a student transfers to a charter school because of fixed costs
    such as insurance, maintenance, and transportation. Instead, public school
    districts must cut expenses – often non-mandated programs and services.
    As a result, unlimited charter school expansion results in lost opportunities
    for students who choose to attend their public school district.

Unlike public school districts that are required to accept and enroll all students,
charter schools can exclude certain students and underserve others. It’s
important to consider student demographics because student demographics
can have implications on student performance, school ratings, and finance.
