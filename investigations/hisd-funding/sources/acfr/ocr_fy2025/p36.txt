ECONOMIC FACTORS AND NEXT YEAR'S BUDGETS AND RATES
The District's General Fund is funded primarily between local property taxes and state revenue. Through the state’s
funding formula, a school district receives a basic allotment for each student with weights added to that allotment
if the student receives additional services such as special education, at-risk (state compensatory education)
bilingual, dyslexia, early education, college, career or military readiness outcomes, career and technology and
transportation, which determines the district's Tier | allotment.
The 2025-2026 General Fund budget, adopted in June 2025, included projected increase of $175.4 million in
revenues and $54.3 million in expenditures. The state revenue projections are based on an estimated average
daily attendance (ADA) of 151,666 or 223,312 based on a weighted average daily attendance (WADA).
In FY2025-2026, HISD's total expenditures are estimated at $2.121 billion.
Actions of the 88th Legislature in 2025:
1.The basic allotment increased to $6,215 for 2025-2026 and 2026-2027. This base amount is generated by
every student in HISD.
2.Increased school safety funding from $10 per ADA to $20 per ADA. Added additional safety funding of
$33,540 per campus
3.Increased the state homestead exemption from $100,000 to $140,000 which was subsequently approved by
voters in November 2025.
4 Reduced the Maximum Compression Rate (MCR) by an additional $0.0194 for tax year 2025, subject to the
90% equity band.
5.Included Maintenance and Operations and Interest Sinking Hold Harmless (covers debt approved by voters
as of September 1, 2025.)
Tax Rate
At budget adoption in June, it was projected that the total district tax rate per $100 of property value would
be $0.8783. This assumed the Maintenance and Operations (M&O rate) of $0.7116 consisting of a maximum
compressed rate of $0.6322 plus five Tier Il “Golden Pennies as well as an interest and sinking (I1&S) rate of
$0.1667. These rates were approved by the Board of Managers in October.
